More than a job title.

I have always been drawn to the things people make with language: the stories they tell, the ideas they defend, the questions they ask and the ways they make sense of the world.

  • since 2023

    On attention

    In February 2023, I decided to take writing seriously as a profession. My first opportunity came through a friend’s brother, who brought me on to write scripts for his YouTube channel. I was researching topics, digging through information and turning what I found into scripts that could keep viewers watching until the end.

    The pay was modest, especially for the amount of work that went into each script, but the experience gave me something I didn’t quite expect: a genuine love for the craft. I began to appreciate the work behind a good script, the impact a well-told story could have and the satisfaction of knowing that something I wrote could hold someone’s attention.

    I kept at it, learned on the job and gradually found my footing. Before the year was over, I had gone from working with one client to juggling several. It was also around this time that I began to realise just how much more I could do with words beyond YouTube scripts. That curiosity would take me into other forms of writing, new industries and entirely different kinds of storytelling.

  • across niches

    On range

    I was juggling multiple clients, navigating different writing niches, and trying to keep up with everything that came with being a teenager in my second year of university.

    Somewhere along the way, other writers, many of them just starting out, began reaching out to me for opportunities to work together. I needed help, but I also saw a chance to create openings for others. I had spent time figuring out my own processes and building systems that worked, so I decided to train and hire three writers, passing on what I had learned and bringing them into client projects.

    Building that team took some of the pressure off me while helping other writers gain practical experience, earn from their skills, and find their footing in the industry.

  • 2025

    On Web3

    In 2025, I took my writing into Web3, starting with writing bounties (and winning a few), exploring communities, and immersing myself in an entirely new ecosystem. Those bounties gave me my first proper taste of crypto money, and let’s just say I liked what I saw. I knew I wanted to stick around.

    I joined Web3 communities, learned from people already building in the space, made valuable connections, and gradually found my footing. That momentum led me to Bitoshi Africa, a financial technology and cryptocurrency platform, where I worked as a Content Writer and Brand Storyteller, leading content development, SEO, research, and brand communication across several African markets.

    Since then, I have stayed active in the space, taking on new roles, partnering with different Web3 teams, and continuously sharpening my craft as the industry evolves. And I’m nowhere near done exploring what this ecosystem has to offer.

  • the rest

    On everything else

    Outside the brief, I follow history, philosophy, science, politics, religion, literature and culture, and the human behaviour that runs through all of them.

The High-Performing Freelancer.

A digital product I created to make practical freelance knowledge more accessible, priced at just ₦2,000.

But the sales figure only tells part of the story.

I built my brand from scratch. I joined classes, learned from people who were further ahead of me, spent hours researching, and gradually pieced together the knowledge that helped me establish myself as a writer and freelancer.

Along the way, I noticed something. Access to information was becoming a business in itself. Industry experts charged for almost every piece of advice, and while there is nothing inherently wrong with charging for expertise, I began to see how easily beginners could be taken advantage of, particularly when much of the information they needed was already available through independent research.

I knew how much work it took to find, understand, and apply that information because I had done it myself. I wanted to make that journey easier for others. I wanted to be part of breaking the barrier of information gatekeeping, not contributing to it.

Over two years, I built a community around writing, freelancing, and personal branding. I hosted X Spaces with over 700 people tuning in and organised free virtual classes that attracted hundreds of registrations. I taught people how to optimise their LinkedIn profiles, build writing portfolios, establish personal brands, and position themselves for opportunities.

My book, The High-Performing Freelancer, was an extension of that commitment: putting practical knowledge within reach of people who were willing to learn and put it to work.
